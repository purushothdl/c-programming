The duty climbs in straight steps. The light does not.

TIM2 makes a 1 kHz square wave on PA5, so the LED never flickers. Every 50 ms main adds 25 to CCR1: 0 to 1000 in 2.05 s, then it starts again. The duty is linear. What your eye receives is a curve.

03 · the numbers

How long should the fade take?

Two constants set the whole ramp: the step size and the wait after each step. The loop writes 0 … 1000 inclusive, so a step that divides 1000 gives 41 writes.

What stays fixed lib/pwm.c

The fader only moves CCR1. PSC, ARR and the PWM mode are set once in pwm_init. The pin path (compare → OC1REF → CC1E → PA5) is on the channels page.

Why the new duty waits for the wrap lib/pwm.c · OC1PE

With OC1PE = 1, a write to CCR1 goes into the preload register and is copied to the active register at the next update event. Without it, the write takes effect at once and can end a pulse early (RM0390 p. 558). That one bit is why a fade never glitches a period.

TIM2->CCMR1 |= ... | TIM_CCMR1_OC1PE;
04 · easy to get wrong

Gotchas

41 writes, not 40duty <= 1000 includes both ends: 0, 25 … 1000 is 41 values, so the cycle is 2 050 ms.
The step must divide 100025, 10 and 50 land on 1000. A step like 30 stops at 990, so the LED never reaches full.
There is no fade downThe inner loop ends and the next pass writes 0. Full to off in one 50 ms step — the jump at the top of every cycle.
Preload keeps pulses wholeWith OC1PE = 1 the new duty is copied at the update event. Without it, a write can end a pulse early or late.
Linear duty, curved lightAt 2.5 % the eye already sees about 19 % of full. Most of the visible change happens in the first half.
PA5 is ownedlib/pwm.c owns TIM2 and PA5. Reconfiguring either elsewhere breaks the fade silently.
